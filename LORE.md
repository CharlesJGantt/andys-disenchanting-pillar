# Lore Seed: Andy's Disenchanting Pillar

The oldest surviving account begins with an ancient warrior called **Fire Dragon** and the theft of his enchanted diamond sword.

According to the story, a rogue witch stole the weapon and placed Curse of Vanishing upon it. Fire Dragon recovered the sword, but victory did not save it. If he fell while carrying it, the weapon and every enchantment bound to it would disappear.

Fire Dragon brought the sword to the Mystics and gave them a challenge: if magic could be bound to an object, then one binding must be removable without destroying all the others. The Mystics accepted his commission. Their enchanters and potion scholars worked together until they produced a pillar that could separate one enchantment and preserve it inside a book.

The first enchantment ever removed was the curse upon Fire Dragon's sword. The sword survived. The curse survived in a book. Nothing was copied, and nothing vanished.

Whether every detail of that story is literal history is unknown. What matters is that the oldest pillar tradition remembers the device not as a weapon, but as an answer to a weapon's unjust fate.

More than twenty years ago, during Andy and Donaldson's first expedition to The Ancient Lands, Donaldson rediscovered a narrow stone monument beneath moss, roots, and collapsed masonry. The expedition first recorded it as a boundary marker. Donaldson reconsidered after noticing that its carved symbols resembled neither a map nor a warning.

He placed a damaged enchanted tool against the lower plinth. A blank book lay among the expedition supplies, and amethyst shards recovered nearby had been set on the stone for sorting. The pillar awakened.

Light moved through the carvings like ink searching for a page. One enchantment lifted from the tool as a spiral of symbols and settled into the waiting book. The tool survived. The enchantment survived. Nothing had been copied.

Donaldson did not claim to understand the device. His surviving notes describe an effect, not an explanation. Later comparison with Mystic sites and the fragmentary Fire Dragon account suggested that the pillar belonged to the same broad tradition of hidden knowledge, potion craft, and enchantment study. Whether the Ancient Lands pillar was an original Mystic device, a later copy, or a parallel reconstruction remains unproven.

Modern CWES reconstructions are called **Andy's Disenchanting Pillars**. They preserve the ancient operating pattern as faithfully as possible:

- a book to receive the separated enchantment,
- amethyst to carry and stabilize its resonance,
- and experience surrendered by the operator to complete the separation.

Mending resists removal because its magic continually repairs the bond between enchantment and object. Curses resist even more strongly, clinging to the history and identity of the item. The greater cost of removing them is not a punishment imposed by the pillar. It is the measure of how tightly the magic refuses to let go.

The most frequently repeated line from Donaldson's field record remains attached to every CWES guide:

> We know what the pillar does. We do not yet know why it obeys.

## Canon note

This document is public-safe seed lore. Donaldson's expedition chronology and the established Mystic themes are canon-grounded. Fire Dragon, the cursed sword, the pillar's origin, the details of its operation, and its connection to specific Mystic guilds remain proposed lore until formally approved.

Additional lore-development notes and draft articles are maintained privately so this public seed remains concise and release-safe.
